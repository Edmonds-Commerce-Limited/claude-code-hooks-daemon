## 15:32 · finding · T1.2 — the instrument for this audit is blind

Measured `verdicts.jsonl` before dispatching anything, on the theory that the
daemon's own decision log should answer "which handlers earn their keep?" —
which is, verbatim, what Plan 00209's config docstring says it was built for.

It cannot. The log is at its 10 MiB cap with 44,180 records, of which 43,929
(99.43%) are status-line renders. Thirteen status handlers each fire ~3,383
times an hour, one render per ~1.15 s, and every record is `allow` because a
renderer has no other verdict to return. Retained window: **65 minutes**.

The dangerous part is not the blindness, it is that the report looks
authoritative. "Total recorded decisions: 44168" reads like history. Its
"Never-fired handlers (59)" list names `prevent-destructive-git` and
`block-sensitive-content` — handlers whose whole value is firing rarely.

Wrote the anti-inference warning into `RESEARCH-verdict-log-is-blind.md` and
put it in every agent brief, because the obvious misuse of that list would be
to delete 59 handlers including the safety net.

## 16:05 · thought · P3 — the removals are the small prize

Plan 00181 already found both loggers and tabulated them `Consumer: NONE`, then
**bounded** them instead of removing them. It also looked straight at `cleanup`
and certified it "the one functioning reaper" without checking the producer
side.

So this shape has now appeared four times and a prior audit walked past three of
them. Bounding an unread log gives you a fixed-size unread log — the bound
answers "will this fill the disk?", which was the question that plan asked, and
never touches "does anyone read this?", which nobody was asking at all.

That reframes the finding. The individual handlers are cheap; the defect is that
"does anything consume this?" is not a question any guard asks continuously, so
it gets answered only when a human happens to wonder. Pushed the judge to make
Phase 3 name that guard rather than list defects.

## 16:10 · decision · P2 — judge overturned seven researcher signals

Fable downgraded seven SUSPECT signals to KEEP and upgraded three the other way.
The `current_time` reasoning is the one worth remembering: it duplicates the
terminal clock, but per Plan 00233's own accounting a removal costs a
`RETIRED_HANDLERS` entry, a config-changes manifest, a truth-changes manifest
and doc updates — more than that handler will ever cost to keep. **The handler
count is not the cost.** An audit that optimises the count instead of the cost
would have deleted it.

Also held the line on FIX-vs-REMOVE: `git_branch` (~6,200 git subprocess spawns
an hour from a 2.0 s TTL resonating against a ~1.15 s render interval) and
`background_process_tracker` (writes records without the `pgid` key its own
harvester reads) are broken features, not pointless ones. Said so explicitly in
the plan so a future reader cannot quietly convert them to deletions.
