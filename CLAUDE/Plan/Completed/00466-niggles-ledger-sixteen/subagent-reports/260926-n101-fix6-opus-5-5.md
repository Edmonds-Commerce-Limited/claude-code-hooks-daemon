## Ruling 2: N107, quote-blind brace pairing (the D-RULE-5 observation)

**Logged** as NIGGLES N107, with a PLAN.md ledger row, and remedied on this branch.

**Proof that it is pre-existing.** Every N107 guard test below is RED on main 4f0a205b3 (19 of 19) and on 1a11131b7.

**Code** (all in `src/claude_code_hooks_daemon/utils/shell_expansion.py`):

- **One scanner.** `_quoted_span_end` decides every span that bash's brace expansion skips:

  - a backslash escape;
  - `'…'`, `"…"`, `$'…'` and `$"…"`;
  - `${…}`;
  - backticks, and `$(…)`, `<(…)` and `>(…)`.

  An unterminated span runs to the end, as its quoting does. Where a span cannot be placed with certainty it raises `UnresolvableBraceQuotingError`, a `TooManyToEnumerateError`. The uncertain cases are a `${…}` holding quotes, escapes or braces, and a `case` word or a heredoc inside `$(…)`.

- **Expansion.** `expand_braces` returns main's quote-blind reading, plus a quote-aware reading (`_quote_aware_brace_expansions`, driven by `_brace_syntax_mask`) whenever the word holds `' " \ $` or a backtick.

  - Each reading has its own cap.
  - The blind reading is kept because a word from `iter_brace_words` may be a fragment of a longer shell word. It also means nothing that denied before is now allowed.
  - An uncertain span raises only if a group is at stake, meaning a `{` other than a `${` with a `}` after it (`_may_hold_a_group`). Otherwise `"${x:-"a"}"` would fail closed on its own.

- **Discovery.** `iter_shell_brace_words` splits text as bash splits it, using the same scanner. It skips comments, reads heredoc bodies by their delimiter lines, and yields the words that hold quoting and braces. It also reads as a command every text a shell may run:

  - substitution bodies;
  - every heredoc body (`bash <<'EOF'`);
  - the substitutions of an unquoted body, where quotes are text;
  - each word after quote removal, which is what `bash -c`, `su -c` and `ssh` receive (only when quote removal changed the word, so recursion always progresses);
  - `eval`'s joined arguments.

  Nesting is bounded by `_MAX_NESTED_SHELL_DEPTH`. Past the bound it raises.

- **Reading those words.** `shell_word_spellings` cuts each word at whitespace outside every matched group, expands each piece on its own under the caps, and yields whitespace-free, quote-removed tokens. A group holding quoted whitespace keeps its piece whole.

  - **Why:** my first version expanded such words whole. Python literals are prose, and read as shell their apostrophes pair across sentences. That produced giant tokens that tripped `_token_mention`'s glob heuristics, multiplied every group past the cap, and moved 10 corpus verdicts. Cutting at whitespace outside every group is sound, because the other streams already report tokens that span whitespace.

- **Code words.** `python_program_streams` returns the program's quote-aware words as `shell_words`, and `BraceExpansionView.shell_words` carries them. `_code_shell_words` applies the same literal-containment rule as `_code_brace_words`.

- **Wiring.** `secret_file_matching._brace_expansion_tokens` runs `iter_shell_brace_words` over every source it already enumerates, plus the view's `shell_words`, through `shell_word_spellings`.
