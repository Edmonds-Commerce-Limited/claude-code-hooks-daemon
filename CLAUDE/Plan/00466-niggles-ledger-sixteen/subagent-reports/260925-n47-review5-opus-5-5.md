## Item 2: the supervisor never fights the human

### Finding 1 (MAJOR): an episode outlives the human's move to a lower family

Reproduction: `probe_n47r5r_scenarios_test.py::test_s1b_human_picks_sonnet_while_backoff_holds_second_episode`. It uses the default `CompactPolicy`, and the host is simulated by calling `mark_model_restore` after each typed `/model`.

1. At T, an attributed downgrade (record `uuid-record-1`) opens an episode. `/model fable` is typed at T and lands.
2. At T+300, the classifier fires again. A new record, `uuid-record-2`, opens a second episode. The 3600 s backoff holds the restore.
3. At T+900, the human gives up on Fable and picks **Sonnet**. The session stays on Sonnet.
4. Output: `S1b typed while human on sonnet: ['/model fable@+3600', ...]`. The supervisor reverts the human's Sonnet pick 45 minutes after they made it.

Variants:

- `test_s1` (`CCY_MODEL_RESTORE_SECONDS=300`): the human picks Sonnet 30 s into the episode, and `/model fable` follows at +300.
- `test_s6` (the default delay of 0): a Haiku pick on the first injectable tick is answered with `/model fable`. This is a race with whoever reaches the idle prompt first.

Cause: `note_model_reading` closes an episode only when `session != ep_session or _family_rank(family) > _family_rank(ep_family)`. A drop BELOW the fallback leaves it open, and the dead `else` branch at `:3764-3767` ("a further drop … follows the session down") shows the design intends this. The same close condition is on `main` (`git show main:.claude/ccy/claude-supervise.py`, line 4057). So this is pre-existing, and under the owner's rules that is not a terminal state. The fix sits in the function this branch rewrote.

Direction:

- Close (and spend) the episode on ANY foreground change the supervisor did not cause, not only on a rise.
- Better, key the episode to the fallback MODEL id (the record carries `fallback_model`). A move from `claude-opus-4-8` to `claude-opus-5-5`, which is a human pick in the same family, would then close it too.
- The one reading that must not close it is the supervisor's own awaited restore (`_restore_awaiting`), which already exists.
- Add payload tests: a Sonnet pick during a backoff-held episode, a Sonnet pick during a delayed episode, and a same-family different-model pick. Delete the unreachable further-drop branch (my mutation S8 survives, finding 4).

### Finding 2 (MINOR): a record is attributed even after a Fable reading has superseded it

In each case below, the human's manual Fable to Opus pick falls within 300 s of the record's event time, and the supervisor types `/model fable`:

| Probe     | Case                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Output                                             |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `test_s2` | The record's event is at T. The supervisor's first reading is at T+10 and already shows Opus (a restart, or stale readings), so no episode opens and nothing is spent. The human picks Fable at T+100 and Opus at T+200                                  | `['/model fable@+200', …]`                         |
| `test_s4` | The block record `uuid-record-1` is restored and spent. Its standalone record then publishes UNPAIRED with its own uuid at T+95. The block had left the 1 MiB tail, or the gap was over 180 s. Fable is seen at T+150, and the human picks Opus at T+200 | `['/model fable@+3620', …]` (once backoff expires) |
| `test_s5` | A legacy signal with no `record_id` and no timestamp. Its key is never spent. The restore lands at +0, and the human picks Opus at +120                                                                                                                  | `['/model fable@+3600', …]`                        |

The fixer's report calls s5 and the legacy-key/uuid mismatch "bounded residuals". Under the owner's rules they are not terminal. All three share one fact the supervisor already observes: it SAW the session on Fable after the record's event time, so that downgrade was already over.

Direction: remember, per session, when a top-family reading was last seen. Refuse attribution when that time is later than the record's `event_ts` plus a small render skew (for example 30 s: the status line can lag the switch by a few renders). That one rule closes s2, s4, s5 and the legacy-to-uuid key change. Add each probe as a payload test.

Probes that did NOT fight:

- a late record of a manual pick (the window rejects it);
- a duplicate republish of a spent id;
- a record for another session;
- a keyless record first seen long ago.

A record_id collision is possible through a byte offset reused after a transcript rewrite or rotation. It only affects entries with no uuid, and it can only make a genuine new downgrade look spent. That is the safe direction (no restore, no fight).
