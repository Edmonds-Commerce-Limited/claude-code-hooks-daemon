## N4 — session-scoped pause for a declared cron (decision 2)

**New CLI verbs.** They are named like the CLI's other hyphenated
`verb-object` commands (`approve-merge`, `clear-goal`):

- `hooks-daemon cron-pause <job> --reason "..."` records a pause.
- `hooks-daemon cron-resume <job>` removes it. Resuming a job that is not
  paused is not an error.

Both verbs refuse three things:

- a job id that is not an ACTIVE `persistent_crons` job. The error lists the
  active ids.
- a missing `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`, because the pause belongs to one session.
- a blank `--reason`. A multi-line reason is collapsed onto one line.

A write failure is reported as "pause not recorded" with exit 1.

**The marker** is in `src/claude_code_hooks_daemon/utils/cron_pause.py`. It is
stored in `cron-pauses.json` under the daemon untracked dir, beside the
human-input blockage marker. It reuses that marker's mechanism:

- **Validity.** `CronPause.is_live` builds a `BlockageMarker` and calls
  `marker_is_valid`, so the two expiring markers share one rule. A pause for
  another session, a future-dated pause or an expired pause is no pause.
- **The write.** The atomic, owner-only write was lifted out of `write_marker`
  as `write_json_atomically`, which raises on failure. `write_marker` still
  fails open around it, while the CLI can report the failure.

`PAUSE_TTL_SECONDS` is 24 hours. Expired entries from every session are pruned
on each write. A read fails open in the enforcing direction: a missing or
corrupt file, or a malformed entry, counts as no pause.

**Enforcers.** The shared verdict is `verdict_for_missing_crons` in
`utils/cron_enforcement.py`. `cron_stop_enforcer` and
`cron_subagent_stop_enforcer` both call it:

- Any missing job that is NOT paused still denies, and the deny text also
  names the live pauses.
- If every missing job is paused, the result is ALLOW with a note naming the
  job, the reason, the expiry (UTC and relative), the `cron-resume` command,
  and `persistent_crons` as the permanent switch.
- The note speaks on the first qualifying stop and then every 10th
  (`PAUSE_ADVISE_INTERVAL`, through the shared `SessionAdviceCounter`). A Stop
  ALLOW with context makes Claude Code continue for one turn, so an unlimited
  note would cost a turn on every stop. The rate-limit key includes the
  pause's timestamp, so a new pause is announced at once.
- A paused job that is actually running produces no note.

**Assertor.** `persistent_cron_assertor` leaves a job paused for the
SessionStart's own session out of the create list. It states the pause
instead, with "Do NOT re-create a paused job in this session." A new session
has a new id, so it is asked for the job again. `persistent_crons` is
unchanged as the only permanent switch.

## N7 — an unattributed effort drop is trusted as manual (decision 3)

This change is in `.claude/ccy/claude-supervise.py`.

- `_latch_unattributed_effort_drop` runs from `note_model_reading`. It latches
  `_manual_effort_active` when a fresh reading shows a LOWER known level than
  the last known level, for the same session and the same family, and the new
  level is not one the supervisor typed.
- `note_own_effort_injection(level)` is called at DECISION time by all three
  armed `/effort` branches: DROP ANCHOR, coupled and floor. Because it runs in
  the worker, it ships by hot-reload. It is not called for dry-run markers.
  The record lasts until a reading shows that level, with no time window.
  That answers the ledger's stated risk: a late status render cannot turn the
  supervisor's own drop into a "human" one. Once consumed, a later human
  return to the same level is latched.
- **Not latched:** a rise, a change across a family change or a session change,
  or an unknown level. An unknown level is no evidence, and the comparison
  resumes from the last known level.
- **One deliberate difference from a typed `/effort`:** an inferred drop does
  NOT cancel an armed coupled correction. A drop seen between a `/model`
  injection and its `/effort` is as likely the switch settling as a human, and
  that correction is what keeps fable off xhigh.
- A consume-once note (`effort X -> Y on <family> was not injected by the supervisor — latched as a manual choice, floor off for this spell`) goes to
  decision.log, the same way as the unattributed-downgrade note.
- The three new fields round-trip through `export_state`/`import_state`.
- **The asymmetry is documented beside both rules:**
  - the new method's docstring
  - a comment at the `_downgrade_is_attributed` branch
  - the module docstring's floor paragraph
  - a paragraph next to the unattributed-downgrade text in
    `docs/guides/HANDLER_REFERENCE.md` (`model_downgrade_recorder`)

**Tests.** `tests/unit/supervise/test_unattributed_effort_drop.py` has 13
cases. They cover:

- the owner's report reproduced (opus, selector to medium): no `/effort high`
- the latch held on later ticks
- the decision.log note
- an anchor drop that is not latched, including across a reporting lag
- a dry-run drop that is latched
- a consumed own injection
- rise, family change, session change and unknown level
- the coupled correction left intact
- the export/import round trip

**RED confirmed**: 7 of the 13 failed before the change. The other 6 are the
negative cases, which already held.
