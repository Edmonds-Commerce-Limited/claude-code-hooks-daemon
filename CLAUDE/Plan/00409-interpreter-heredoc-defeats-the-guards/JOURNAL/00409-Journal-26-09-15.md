## 00:24 · finding · — — being precise about what the reviewer got wrong

Read the report properly rather than relying on the earlier summary of it, and
the correction is narrower and fairer than "the reviewer was wrong".

What it says, verbatim: *"**Not a regression:** `destructive_git` has the
identical hole ... So this is a codebase-wide class, not something N12
introduced, and it does not block the release."*

The first half is CORRECT and I should not have implied otherwise: commit
`a391132e` (N12) did not introduce this. The error is the inference. The
reviewer's brief was to review N12's diff, so "not introduced by N12" was the
question actually asked — but the RELEASE question is "has behaviour changed

(Plan 00377 N7) added the blanking earlier in the same unreleased range.
Checking `destructive_git` and finding the same hole correctly proved the class
was wider than N12, and "wider than N12" was then read as "pre-existing". It
does not follow, and nothing in the diff under review could have shown it —
only the shipped tag could.

Two things follow that are worth keeping:

1. The reviewer found this, rated it *"the highest-value fix available"*, and
   was right about that. The finding is theirs; only the severity call was mine
