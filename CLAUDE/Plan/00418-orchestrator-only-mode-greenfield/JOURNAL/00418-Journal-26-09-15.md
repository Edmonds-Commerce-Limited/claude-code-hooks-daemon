## Task 2.1 — the half that was a build, not a decision

I had recorded Task 2.1 as owner-gated and told the supervisor so. Re-reading
its own text, it offers two routes: "record enough to classify Bash, or draw
the boundary on `Write`/`Edit` alone". Only the second is a decision. The first
is work, and it is the work that makes the decision answerable from evidence
rather than taste.

Gathering the evidence does not depend on which boundary is eventually chosen,
so it was the thing to do first — and it forecloses nothing: drawing the line
on `Write`/`Edit` alone is exactly as available afterwards as it was before.

The implementation got much smaller once I stopped designing and went looking.
My first plan was a bespoke classification ledger under `untracked/`: a new
JSONL, a write lock, a retention cap, its own aggregation. Then I read
`HandlerVerdict`:

> rule: Optional handler-set sub-classification (`HookResult.rule`), e.g.
> pipe_blocker's "blacklisted" vs "unknown".

The field already existed, for exactly this, and `verdicts.jsonl` already
records it. So the whole feature is one `rule=` argument plus a pure
classifier. No new log, no new file, no new retention policy, and the existing
`hooks-daemon verdicts` reads it. The cheaper design was not a compromise —
it was the one that was already there.

**Heads only, and the label is capped.** `classify_bash_command` emits command
heads (`git status`, `pytest`, `test.bash`) and never an argument. That is the
privacy floor first: the label lands in a log, so it must not become a channel
for tokens, hostnames or paths, and a test asserts directly that a
`--token abcdef123456 --host internal.example.com` command labels as `deploy`.
It is also the right granularity — `git status` versus `git commit` is the
distinction the boundary turns on, and nothing further along the command line
can inform it.

Two details that are easy to get wrong and would have silently ruined the
record:

- **`cd /workspace && <real command>` is the dominant shape in this repo.**
  Labelling that "cd" would have erased almost the whole tally. `cd`/`pushd`/
  `popd` are transparent: the segment is skipped, not labelled.
- **Cardinality.** The label is aggregated, so an uncapped compound command
  would mint a unique bucket per invocation and the tally would count nothing.
  Deduped in first-seen order, capped at three heads, then `…`.

Anything the naive segment split mishandles becomes `<other>` rather than
being echoed — withholding a label costs one uninformative row, emitting raw
text costs the privacy property.
